Economic and applied policy analysis

Illegal handel ↗ (Undeclared imports)

The study showed that the lost public tax income due to undeclared imports amounted to some 0.25 bn dkk. I particularly liked this study because it was taken up by the Danish Parliament and the Ministry of Taxation, which had previously been dismissive of the notion that undeclared imports were of any substantial magnitude that required a policy response

Private sundhedsforsikringer ↗ (Private healthcare insurance)

Controversially, in the Danish publicly financed healthcare system employer-paid healthcare auxiliary insurance has been gaining ground. The study, drawing on individual-level micro-data, showed that private healthcare reduces employee sick leave absence, yielding a net value added of around 1.8-2.6 bn dkk, whilst benefiting public finances by some 0.9-1.2 bn dkk

Gymnasieanalysen / Undervisningseffekt-analysen ↗ (High school education effect study)

What is the effect of the education received at a particular high school? If we are to utilize best practice and systematically improve quality, we need to be able to asses the quality of high school education. This, however, is rendered difficult by the fact that school grades are affected by a variety of factors so that schools where grade averages are lo also tend to have more students with sociodemographic backgrounds that suggest lower outcomes e..g the grade average from primary school. This study employed comprehensive indvidual level microdata to control away these effects and distill the "education effect" for each indiviual high school in the country.

EU's Persondataforordning ↗ (Impact assessment of the GDPR for Danish businesses)

Impact assessment of the cost of compliance with GDPR, showing aggregate costs for Danish businesses at around 8 bn dkk. I really liked this study, not least because it took the front page of the Danish Financial Times-equivalent, Børsen

Amazons indtog i Norden ↗ (Amazon's potential entry into the Nordics)

Amazon's impending entry into the Nordics has long been the talk of the town, so drawing on research of Amazon's presence in other markets the study laid out scenarios for what market share Amazon might feasibly capture in Denmark. Whilst Amazon has an online market share of around 50% in the US market, as a share of total retail turnover a likely scenario is still only a market share of a few percent for Amazon

Hård konkurrence i detailhandlen ↗ (Intense competition in the retail sector)

The study was conducted as a response to a research project carried out by the Danish Consumer Authority which spurred a public debate about Danish retail sector prices ostensibly being "too high". However, not only does that run counter to common intuition in a country with a multitude of international discount chains having attempted entry, the study essentailly rested on a comparison with eight countries that did not establish econometric controls for different wage levels. Unsurprising, when we reproduced the agency's study but introduced a control for wage levels, the price level in Danish retail provded to be, if anything, too low.

Skattetrykket er ikke ændret i 30 år ↗ (The tax burden has not changed substantially in 30 years)

What was great about the study was that it, by rather simply highlighting publicly available data, provided a strong "myth-buster" against the consensus belief in public discourse that the centre-right majority through the 2000's had somehow passed massive tax cuts; when in fact the tax system had been overhauled and rendered more efficient whilst keeping the overall tax burden more or less constant

Investeringsgap på 413 mia. kr. ↗ (Investment gap)

Danish investments abroad has long eclipsed investments from abroad into Denmark, leading to an "investment gap" of around 400 bn euro. Through a fixed-effect estimation we show that if an OECD country reduces its corporate tax rate by one percentage point relative to other countries, foreign investments increase by 1.5 percent the ensuing year and 1.4 percent the year after

Eksportarbejdspladser i service ↗ (Export jobs in the services sector)

The traditional belief (going back to how the Balassa-Samuelson effect is presented in your Macro101) is that the manufacturing sector is the exporting sector which is hugely important for the country's ability to buy stuff it wants from abroad, whilst the services are more domestically oriented and may have prices above what would prevail if they could be easily traded over a distance. However, drawing on input-output industry-level data, the study showed that the largest number of export jobs is actually in the service sector. If we include second-order exporting jobs i.e. jobs that supply exporting companies the importance of the service industries for international trade becomes even more apparent.

Julehandelsprognose ↗ (Christmas retail forecast)

Consumer mood is important in affecting private consumption and hence the economy, and the Christmas sales provide a way to gauge how propensity to spend it changing. And more to the point to be honest, it was just one of the greatest media hits of the year since every news outlet in the country wanted to report on what we forecasted for the Christmas sales. Sometimes you just have to be part of the discussion, then you can come with your more policy- og economic- related studies and messages afterwards!

Online-salg af dagligvarer (Online grocery sales)

Ecommerce with groceries in 2017 totalled around 2.5 bn dkk, corresponding to some 2% of total retail turnover, the study showed. As a result-oriented person, what I really like about this piece of research was that it was taken up as the front page of the leading national newspaper Politiken!

Digital uddannelse (Digital skills and education)

Sometimes very simple studies are great if you have the right idea. This proved the point with large traction in news media and among policymakers. Every third Danish adult feels they do not have been sufficiently prepared ("klædt godt nok på") for what is needed on the labour market in terms of digital skills by their education. The result holds for young i.e. people that have graduated in recent years. Policy implication: The dearth of ICT skills holds back our businesses, derailing our productivity, and the educational system has not done enough to adapt to these new needs

Digital økonomi ↗ (Digital economy)

This piece of research took stock of the digital economy in Denmark, trying to estimate its size and highlighting key facts and developments.

Skattereform: Selskabsskat på 19% ↗ (Economic impact of reducing corporate tax to 19%)

Reducing corporate tax to 19% will increase GDP by 8.5 bn dkk. For a typical family that corresponds to 2.500 dkk extra per year. While admittedly simply reporting the results from a study carried out by public authorities as a response to a question asked to the minister in parliament, the piece highlights the importance of taking publicly available calculations and introducing them in the right context.

Frihandel (Free trade)

The study was primarily a compilation of facts and other studies that show the value and merit of global free trade. Debunking some of the criticism that trade liberalization gives rise to greater inequality, it was meant to help out an International Trade Division that was supposed to do advocacy work on the issue but possessed too little economic understanding, not so ideal!!

Sommerhusreglen ↗ (The Danish Summer Home Rule)

Unbeknownst to most, Denmark has secured an opt-out from the common EU rules in that only people with permanent residence can legally buy a summer home. The study looked at the state of play and considered the Netherlands as a possible point of comparison without such restrictions. What I particularly like about the study is that it is just a good example of how to draw up a paper with the background facts, messages and perspectives that will be useful for decisionmakers while also getting the message across. Oh, and because it was singled out as an example of a useful paper by an MP!

Den digitale dansker (The digital Dane)

Ok so honestly I didn't like this study at all, and thought it was fundamentally uninteresting to spend so many resources showing how some Danes are more digital (however we define it) than others. But then again I am no fan of survey research. At any rate since it was a comprehensive study that some people felt strongly about it should probably be highlighted.

Højere kvalitet når private løser opgaverne (Quality when private providers deliver welfare services)

Adopting what I thought was an interesting new avenue for research, the study considered how recipients of welfare services rate what they receive, e.g. kindergarten, school, retirement home. On the aggregate, among people that use private-sector actors 82% report they are satisfied or very satisfied, whilst the same only holds for 75% of public sector users.

Den offentlige konkurrence skal styrkes med 60 mia. kr. (Increasing competition in public procurement)

THe study considered the economic effects of increasing the amount of public sector activity open to a tender proces where private-sector actors may win the contract by 60 bn dkk. Based on previous experiences an average gain of 8 bn dkk is foreseen, depending on the areas chosen, but at least 3 bn dkk if the areas where realized gains historically have been lowest is chosen as the point of reference.

Kredittørke ↗ (Credit drought)

Drawing on data from the Danish Central Bank and a company survey, the report, whilst not exceptional in what it does, gave what I thought was a pretty good exposé highlighting all the relevant facts and information about one of the key questions in the aftermath of the financial crisis.

Kommunale vindere i uddannelseskapløbet ↗ (Municipal winners in the education race)

The average national length of education has increased by 3.3 months over the past 7 years, but with considerable municipal differences.

Ideernes land ↗ (Land of ideas)

Drawing on company-level microdata, we show that companies that have innovation activites on average are 6% more productive (in terms of value added per employee) than other companies. The implication is that if we for example could increase the share of companies with innovation activities from current 44% to 50%, we would expect total value added in the Danish economy to inrease by 4.3 bn dkk.

Realkreditternes bidragssatser bør falde ↗ (Mortgage administration fees should come down)

Average administrative fees on mortgages have increased from 0.37% to 0.76% from 2009 to 2015, which has led to an aggreagate extra expense around 9 n dkk for mortgage holders, controlling for the increase in total number of loans made.

Svensk niveau for investeringer ↗

We did plenty of these country-analysis style papers. Sometimes more elaborate, sometimes just the key numbers and facts. This piece I like because it also gives the whole background and impact assesments from the Danish Foreign Ministry and the EU Commission of concluding an FTA with Japan.

Det rigtige uddannelsesvalg ↗ (The right educational choice)

the Danish educational system spends 600 million dkk yearly paying for a high school education to people who decide to pursue a vocational degree afterwards. While perhapsnot entirely wasted, public resources could be saved if more young people made the right choice the first time.

Gebyr ved udeblivelser ↗ (No-show fees in healthcare)

Perhaps hard fathom for the international audience, but Denmark has free helathcare and not even a no-show fee in primary healthcare. The study assessed the likely implications of introducing a 250 dkk no-show fee, and suggested public income of around 400 million dkk is likely

Frokostpause eller velfærd? ↗ (Lunch break or more welfare?)

80% of Danish public sector employee have paid lunch break. Increasing their working time by 2.5 hours with full wage compensation, bringing their effective working time up to the 37 hours that is the norm on the private sector labour market, would increase GDP by 1.4%, and increase the total supply of labour by the equivalent of 48.000 full-time employees.

Millioner at spare ved at reducere sygefraværet ↗ (Millions can be saved by reducing sickness absence)

The average number of sick days per muniicpal employee varies between municipalities from 9.5 to 16.3, considerably higher than in the private sector. Even comparing identical positions reveal considerably higher sick leave abesenteeism in the municiapl sector. The total cost associated with municipal sick leave is estimated at 9.2 bn dkk. If municipalities couldfor example reduce sick leave down to the average for the 25% best performers it will yield a 1.3 bn dkk gain

Stor gevinst ved at få flere højtuddannede til den private sektor ↗ (Gains from increasing tertiary-educated employment in the private sector)

A large number of tertiary degree holders find employment in the public sector, whilst the private sector experiences a shortage of highly specialized labour. In a scenario where the average level of education in the private sector was increased to the same level as the public sector, GDP is estimated to become 77 bn dkk higher.

Lav belønningsgrad i Danmark ↗ (Low “reward factor” in Denmark)

The study looed at the compensation received for people who become highly productive labour. It noted that marginal tax rates are very high in Denmark in international comparison, that the average percentage income increase associated with obtaining tertiary education was very low in international comparison, and only a third of Danish companies set wages largely or completely based on performance (of course to a largeextent reflecting collective bargaining).

København er Sydsveriges hovedstad ↗ (Copenhagen, the capital of Southern Sweden)

Surveys have their uses. But if you want to go above the daily grind run-of-the-mill analysis, a special sample is often a way to do interesting stuff. Within a 12 months period 71% of the people sampled in Southern Sweden had been to Copenhagen, as opposed to 43% who had visited Stockholm.

33 mia. at spare hvis Danmark kan efterligne Finlands uddannelsessystem ↗ (Potential savings from emulating the Finnish education system)

The FInnish educational system outperforms Denmark, yet as a percentage of GDP it is less costly. If the percent of GDP cost of the Danish system could be brought down to the FInnish level - which presumably can be done whilst simultanously improving quality - it would save some 33 bn dkk

It kapital har øget produktiviteten ↗ (ICT capital has boosted productivity)

hTe report presented figures describing how ICT capital investments have contributed to productivity growth over he past decades

Det offentlige forbrug er 24,5 mia. større end normalt ↗ (Public expenditure is DKK 24.5 billion above its historical average)

By 2015 Danish public epxenditure totallet 26.2% of GDP, against a historical average of 24.9% - i.e. 24.5 bn dkk more than if it had been at the historical average - and well above the 25.3% the Social Democratic government wanted when it held power for a prolonged period of time throughout the nineties.

Fuld momsrefusion vil give mellem 1.300 og 2.600 jobs ↗ (Full VAT refund could create 1,300–2,600 jobs)

Expanding VAT exemptions for businesses purchasing hospitality services would increase employment by 1.300-2600 by rendering eDnmark more competitive vis-a-vis Sweden as a place hosting large events and conferences and generally spurring more firm spending (yes, Bo should perhaps have rounded the figures and there is a question of what the long-term structural effect will be, but it was not a bad piece in and of itself)

Den genindførte grænsekontrol mellem Danmark og Sverige har medført en række negative konsekvenser for en væsentlig andel af de danske virksomheder i regionen

Blodfattig højkonjunktur kalder på reformer ↗ (Anemic upturn calls for reforms)

The study called into attention that if we consider growth per capita - taking into account a considerable demographic increase - rather than solely focusing on GDP growth, then economic growth has actually been unimpressive. GNI per capita remains below the late-2007 level, and if it had followed a historical trend every person would on avergae have 46.000 dkk higher income today.

Selskabsskatteudligning svækker incitament til erhvervsvenlighed ↗ (Corporate-tax equalisation weakens incentives for business-friendly policy)

REvenues from corporate tax is redistributed between municipalities. This lessens the incentive to ensure a business-friendly municipal busienss climate. A total of 1.1 bn dkk is redistrubted. In some municipalities close to 50% of the revenue is transferred to other municipalities, whilst other municipalities receive up to 345% of the original revenue throguh transfers.
